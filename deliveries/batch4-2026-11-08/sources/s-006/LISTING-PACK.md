## FAQ（Etsy FAQ 欄逐條填）

**Q: How does the catch-up / re-spread work?**
A: Every payday row shows a "Set aside" amount. It is always worked out from two live numbers: what is still left to save, and how many paydays you have left. If you miss a payday (type 0) or save less than planned, the amount left simply gets divided across the paydays that remain, so the following amounts adjust themselves. The goal total never changes - only the split does. If you save more than planned, the later amounts come down instead.

**Q: I am paid monthly. Does it still work?**
A: Yes. Choose "Monthly" in the "How often are you paid?" cell and the schedule is built from your real payday dates (the sheet steps month to month from your first payday). Monthly paydays fall on the same day each month; in a shorter month, that month's payday is its last day. The same applies to weekly and every-2-weeks pay - the plan always counts paydays, not calendar weeks.

**Q: Does it work in Google Sheets and in Excel?**
A: Both. The file is a standard .xlsx: open it directly in Microsoft Excel, or upload it to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets (then File > Make a copy, so the copy is yours to edit). Every formula is a plain spreadsheet formula - no macros, no add-ons, nothing to install. A Start Here page in the kit walks through both, step by step.

**Q: It is already November / December. Is it too late to start?**
A: The countdown is built for late starts - it only ever uses the paydays you actually have left, even if that is just one or two. The amounts per payday will be bigger, but the maths and the catch-up rule work exactly the same.

**Q: Do I have to print anything?**
A: No. The spreadsheet is the product and works fully on screen. The printable check-off page is there if you like a paper version on the fridge - one page, black & white, in A4 and US Letter.

**Q: What dates does it cover?**
A: Any. The example dates start in late October 2026 with a save-by date of Dec 24, but every date is yours to change - the schedule rebuilds from your first payday.
